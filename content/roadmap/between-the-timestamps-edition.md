## A timeline is not a conclusion

Digital forensic work often arranges records into a sequence, but a clean-looking order can hide awkward questions. What generated each timestamp? Was it stored in UTC, local time, or another representation? Did the source clock drift? Could two records describe the same event with different precision, delay, or uncertainty?

A row of timestamps does not answer those questions by itself. **A sequence is not yet an explanation.**

## Why the gaps matter

The illustration uses three deliberately offset, abstract time axes. Solid ticks suggest available observations while hatched intervals and empty space call attention to provenance differences, unknowns, and missing context.

None of the marks depicts a real log, case, device, source-clock offset, or investigative finding. This is a conceptual editorial picture, not an analytical method, software dashboard, or worked forensic comparison.
