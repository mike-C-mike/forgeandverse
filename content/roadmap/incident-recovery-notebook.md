## Incident work creates temporary knowledge

During an active incident, people know things that are true for ten minutes, thirty minutes, or until the next change.

A notebook can help hold that layer as long as everyone remembers the boundary: temporary working notes do not replace the incident record, ticket, approved communications path, or evidence-preservation process.

## Three structured moments

The first dummy gives structure only to incident orientation, a decision log, and a recovery checkpoint.

Everything else is ordinary writing space.

That keeps the notebook useful for timelines, diagrams, host relationships, questions, meeting notes, hypotheses, and the other shapes technical reasoning takes before it becomes clean documentation.

## The next honest gate

The first bound proof needs to be used in a tabletop or lab incident. If the structured pages are skipped, that is evidence. If responders want more boxes, that is evidence too. The product follows the work rather than forcing the work to follow the product.
