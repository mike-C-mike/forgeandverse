## The moment is before the shelf

A surprising amount of evidence-room friction begins with a small unanswered question at intake.

An identifier is incomplete. The description and package do not quite reconcile. A special storage need is implied but not obvious. A discrepancy that could have been resolved in thirty seconds becomes a phone call two days later.

The card is meant to serve that moment.

## A review aid, not a universal policy

Property and evidence procedures vary by agency, evidence type, local law, accreditation practice, facility design, and the systems used to document custody.

That makes a universal packaging checklist the wrong product.

The better object is a compact review card that asks whether the local requirements have been satisfied and whether anything still needs to be resolved while the submitting person is available.

## The first structure

The first pass is organized around seven questions:

1. Can the item be tied cleanly to the correct case or incident?
2. Does the submitted description match what is actually being received?
3. Is packaging or seal condition something that needs to be documented under local policy?
4. Is the transfer source and receiving context clear?
5. Does local policy require special storage, hazard handling, or another exception?
6. Is there any discrepancy that should be resolved before routine storage?
7. What is the immediate destination or next action?

The final card should be faster than reading a procedure manual and much less authoritative than one.

## The release boundary

The card should never tell a user how to package a firearm, dry biological material, handle narcotics, count currency, or process another specialized evidence type. Those instructions belong to the agency's approved procedures.

Forge & Verse can help the custodian remember to ask the right question. It should not invent the answer.

## The next honest gate

The next version needs a real layout and a desk simulation using several ordinary intake scenarios. If the prompts feel repetitive, policy-specific, or too slow at the counter, they get cut before this becomes a printable release.
