## The moment it serves

An examination rarely pauses at a clean boundary. A tool may still be running. A question may be half answered. The next action may be obvious at 4:47 p.m. on Friday and strangely absent by Monday morning.

The Bench Status Pad is meant for that gap. It gives the examiner one small place to capture the active state of the work before leaving the bench.

## What belongs on the sheet

The page records only what is needed to re-enter the examination quickly: where the process stands, what finished last, what comes next, what is blocking progress, and the one detail most likely to vanish over the break.

The first proof also tests three simple state markers: whether a process remains running, whether the work is safe to resume, and whether the examination is blocked. Those markers must prove useful in practice or disappear.

## What it is not

This is not a permanent examination note, chain-of-custody record, or substitute for agency documentation. It is a short-lived continuity aid. Because even temporary notes may contain case information, the pad carries a clear reminder to secure and dispose of each sheet according to policy.

## What the proof must answer

The question is no longer whether the idea fits on a page. It does. The question is whether the page fits the end of a real shift.

The next pass should be completed with an actual case scenario, normal handwriting, and the mild impatience of someone trying to leave the office. Any field that slows that moment without returning useful context has not earned its space.
