## The question comes first

A phone, laptop, or account can hold an enormous amount of information without answering the thing an investigator needs to establish. Authority and scope define what can be examined; the investigative objective gives the work direction.

That is the subject of this piece. Its central line is not a claim that the device is unimportant. It is a reminder that the object and the question are not interchangeable.

## Why the illustration is deliberately incomplete

The offset frames and abstracted screen fragments suggest a device full of possible artifacts. None of those marks stands for an actual examination record, extracted message, case identifier, or tool result.

The wider contrast between the paper-colored statement and the dark illustration keeps the device visibly present without making it the hero. The eye should return to the question.

## What has and has not been completed

The editorial composition has been reviewed and approved for publication as a **development study**. The visible artwork is a screen illustration, not a print specification. A small vector-led production master and physical material tests would be separate future steps, with their own approval gates.

The underlying thought is explored further in the published [The Device Is Not the Question](/journal/the-device-is-not-the-question/) practice note.

## The next honest gate

Test a true vector-first 12 x 8 composition with this hierarchy before commissioning a material proof. Confirm edge clearance, typography at realistic reading distances, and whether the device drawing adds meaning rather than merely decorating the line.
