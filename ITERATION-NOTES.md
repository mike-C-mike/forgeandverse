# Iteration 6: Curation and Utility

This round removes artificial catalog growth and gives the first free release a real operational reason to exist.

## Public archive

- Reduced the Work archive to the one art piece currently believed in: **Believe in the Badge**
- Removed draft art concepts that were serving as category filler
- Removed the repeated Recent Work block from the homepage so a single featured work is not shown twice
- Hid archive filters until enough work exists to make filtering useful
- Added single-work archive styling and suppressed empty related-work sections

## Wall-edition direction

- Reframed Believe in the Badge as an ornate wall-edition study
- Added material intent to Work metadata and detail pages
- Added internal review standards for metal, archival, acrylic, and mounted presentation
- Established that material, finish, crop, scale, and mounting are part of the design

## First useful free release

Replaced the decorative verification card and wallpaper with a two-page **Digital Forensics Intake Request**.

The release includes:

- print-ready PDF
- editable DOCX
- requestor, case, authority, scope, device, target-data, handling, and forensic-unit acceptance fields
- explicit reminders to state the investigative question and use an approved method for credentials
- local-adaptation guidance and internal-use permission

The DOCX and PDF were rendered and visually reviewed as a two-page document.

## On the Anvil

The public workbench now contains only three credible items:

- Believe in the Badge ornate wall edition
- Bench Status Pad, a 4 x 6 tear-away continuity reminder
- Examination Notebook, structured around actual examiner workflow

## Internal governance

Added:

- `CURATION-STANDARD.md`
- `BENCH-STATUS-PAD-BRIEF.md`
- `DIGITAL-FORENSICS-INTAKE-RELEASE-NOTES.md`

The governing rule is simple: one strong piece is better than a category full of acceptable ones.
