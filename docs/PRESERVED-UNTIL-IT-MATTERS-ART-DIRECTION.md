# Preserved Until It Matters Art Direction

## V20 reset

The first composition proved the core thought but gave the teddy bear too much narrative authority. The object became the story instead of the system of custody that allowed a future story to remain possible.

The v20 direction removes the teddy bear as the hero.

## Subject

Custody over time.

The wall piece should recognize that property and evidence personnel preserve meaning before anyone knows which object will matter later.

## Visual grammar

Use:

- horizontal shelf architecture
- generic evidence labels
- seals and retained-item markings
- one ordinary, anonymous focal package or item
- repeated identifiers without real case numbers
- restrained evidence of age and elapsed time
- low-contrast depth rather than crime-scene drama

Avoid:

- teddy-bear sentimentality as the central device
- yellow tape
- weapon silhouettes
- fingerprint overlays
- glowing evidence tags
- invented case files that look real
- agency identifiers
- borrowed details from a real anecdote or case
- decorative "forensic" textures that do not represent custody

## Language hierarchy

Primary:

- `Preserved Until It Matters`

Secondary:

- `Preservation gives truth time to catch up.`

The secondary line should remain only if it still adds meaning once the visual direction becomes quieter.

## Material direction

The next full composition should be tested on:

- archival paper for a quieter, documentary feel
- low-sheen metal only if dark detail and labels remain controlled under office lighting

The two formats should not exist merely to create variants. Each should make the work feel meaningfully different in the room.
