# Product Map

Forge & Verse organizes releases by the need they fill, not by a quota of commodity formats.

## Workbench

Practical tools for the job:

- intake forms
- status and continuity pads
- reference aids
- checklists
- structured notebooks
- handoff sheets
- inventory and custody templates

A Workbench release must reduce friction, preserve context, improve a handoff, or make a recurring task clearer.

## Workspace

Objects for the practitioner:

- tactile notebooks and journals
- desk pads
- mugs
- pen trays
- small desk objects
- workspace organizers
- profession-specific humor

A Workspace release should recognize a real preference, ritual, frustration, or personality. A logo on a generic object is not enough.

## Editions

Art and reminders with enough weight to occupy a room:

- metal wall editions
- archival prints
- mounted works
- substantial typography and image compositions
- professional recognition pieces

An Edition must remain meaningful after the novelty of seeing it for the first time has passed. Material, scale, finish, and mounting are part of the work.

## Release test

Every release should help someone:

1. do the work,
2. inhabit the work, or
3. remember why the work matters.

Each public page should answer:

- Who is this for?
- What need does it fill?
- Why does it exist?
- How is it meant to be used or experienced?

Empty space in the archive is acceptable. Filler is not.
