# Curation Standard

Forge & Verse does not publish to fill categories or satisfy a release quota.

## Core rule

One strong piece is more valuable than a shelf of acceptable ones.

A work earns a public page only when the studio believes in the idea and the form carrying it. A discipline may remain quiet for months without being treated as incomplete.

## Art and wall editions

Wall pieces should be developed as complete objects, not slogans dropped onto generic backgrounds.

Review:

- composition and distance readability
- typography as part of the artwork
- material suitability, including metal, archival paper, acrylic, or other mounting
- edge treatment and crop behavior
- surface finish and reflected light
- scale in a real office, lab, hallway, or briefing room
- whether the piece still carries weight without a product mockup around it

## Free works

A free release must provide real value. It should save time, improve a workflow, express a specific professional identity, or be genuinely worth displaying.

A section does not need filler. Free describes access, not quality.

## Useful objects

Notebooks, pads, and desk objects begin with a real behavior or recurring problem. The inside structure matters more than a themed cover.

## Outside inspiration

Examples supplied for tone, audience, or market context do not become Forge & Verse concepts. Borrowed phrases and recognizable outside ideas stay outside the archive unless rights and authorship are clearly established.
