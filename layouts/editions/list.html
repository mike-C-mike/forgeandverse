What exists, and what does not

From screen to substance.

A design can look finished long before it exists as an object. These are four different kinds of evidence behind a physical edition—not four claims that a product is available.

  1. Concept · Room mockup
    01 / Concept

    Digital mockup

    Helps explore intended placement, proportion, and atmosphere. It cannot demonstrate real material, glare, edge, or mounting.

    Illustrated example: Believe in the Badge · Law Enforcement.

    ● Existing visual study See the edition study →
  2. Design file · Not a physical proof
    02 / Print test

    Production file

    Artwork prepared for a first print or material test. The file gives a specification to test, not evidence that a sample has been made.

    Existing file: A Hash Is a Promise · Digital Forensics.

    ● File prepared for first test See the production study →
  3. ◇ Awaiting real sample photograph
    03 / Physical test

    Physical proof

    A received, manufactured sample that can actually be inspected for color, finish, legibility, edge, and construction.

    Evidence needed: A documented sample photographed after receipt and hands-on review.

    ○ No reviewed sample documented
  4. ◇ Reserved for approved edition
    04 / Approval

    Approved edition

    A configuration reviewed against actual physical results and approved for production. Approval alone does not open checkout.

    Evidence needed: Documented approval and authentic product photography.

    ○ No approved physical edition documented

These examples are from different professional collections. They illustrate the kinds of evidence available, not four consecutive versions of one product. The last two panels remain intentionally empty until real work justifies a photograph.

Evidence is not availability. Individual work pages still use the site's public Development, Production, and Archive states. Only an edition explicitly marked Available may provide customer checkout. How approval and external fulfillment work →
