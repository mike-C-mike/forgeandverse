From concept to customer · An illustrated guide

The handoff is the point.

The studio designs and approves the work. A production specialist makes test samples and, only if an edition is approved and offered, fulfills customer orders.

Read the process in plain text
  1. Design — Forge & Verse: Defines the audience, artwork, intended size, material, finish and construction. Specialists may advise on manufacturing feasibility.
  2. Physical proof — Production specialist: Makes a test sample to the studio's specifications. This is not a public checkout or available edition.
  3. Studio review — Forge & Verse: Inspects and handles the actual sample, requests revisions as needed and approves a configuration only after it meets the studio's criteria.
  4. Customer ordering — External partner: Only after that approved configuration is marked Available does the work page show an identified external checkout. The partner then manages payment, manufacturing, shipping, order status and returns.

What qualifies a physical proof

Approval has to be earned physically.

An edition can be called production-approved only after the studio receives and reviews the real object. A render, mockup, or vendor preview cannot substitute for a physical sample.

What gets reviewed
  • Print and color behavior
  • Surface and glare
  • Reading distance and usable scale
  • Paper, binding, edge, mount, or construction
  • Packaging and arrival condition where relevant
